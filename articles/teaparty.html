The Great Missing T Scandal of Britain

Scholars uncover curious linguistic fallout from the Boston Tea Party

Boston, MA — In a surprising turn for both historians and linguists, a recent study by the “Colonial Language Recovery Project” claims that Britain’s gradual disappearance of the letter T in spoken English dates back to none other than the Boston Tea Party of 1773.

According to the research, British citizens were so scandalized by the dumping of tea into Boston Harbor that the nation collectively vowed to “never again properly pronounce i’.” Records suggest that within weeks, dockworkers in London were overheard dropping their T’s in protest, leading to what experts now call the “Grammatical Grudge of 1773.”

“It star’ed small — people said ‘bo’l’ instead of ‘bottle’,” explained lead researcher Dr. Paige Turner. “Bu’ before long, the whole country was le’in’ i’ slide. I’ was linguisti’ revenge.”

Modern Britons, many of whom still omit the letter when speaking, remain largely unaware of this colonial connection. “I always though’ i’ was jus’ an accen’,” said one baffled Londoner. “Didn’ realize i’ was poli’ical.”

Historians are reportedly thrilled at the discovery, though Americans remain conflicted. “We dumped their tea and apparently their T’s,” quipped one Bostonian. “Seems fair trade to me.”
